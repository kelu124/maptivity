## **CONTEXTUAL FOUNDATION**

### **Specific Local Challenge Addressed:**
King's Landing faces significant environmental challenges, particularly flooding risks from rising sea levels and heavy rains. The Neighborhood Assessment Report highlights the need for innovative solutions to manage stormwater effectively while enhancing recreational spaces (Executive Summary). Currently, areas near Blackwater Bay are neglected and vulnerable, lacking adequate green infrastructure to address flooding concerns. The transformation of these areas into resilience parks will combat flooding while adding much-needed green space and recreational opportunities for residents.

### **Local Assets Leveraged:**
The project will capitalize on King’s Landing's existing natural shoreline along Blackwater Bay. The city's rich tapestry of community networks, with residents already engaged in protecting and celebrating their environment, will be harnessed to foster a sense of stewardship and ownership over the new parks. Local gardening clubs, environmental groups, and schools can play a vital role in establishing plant selections, educational programs, and volunteer initiatives, amplifying existing community efforts to care for and enhance shared spaces.

### **Cultural/Social Fit:**
The Bayfront Resilience Parks initiative aligns with the local values of community connection and historical reverence. Outdoor spaces have long been integral to social life in King's Landing, serving as essential gathering spots for festivals and celebrations. This project will not only protect the environment but will also respect the lively traditions and stories that make King's Landing unique, using natural features as storytelling elements. As locals come together to create and use these spaces, they will enhance social cohesion and cultural pride.

## **PROJECT DESCRIPTION**

### **Core Concept:** 
The Bayfront Resilience Parks will transform under-utilized waterfront areas into vibrant, green spaces that serve dual purposes: managing stormwater effectively and creating recreational hubs for community interaction. These parks will integrate green infrastructure features like rain gardens, wetlands, native plant landscaping, and educational spaces, creating immersive environments that enhance ecological resilience while inviting community engagement.

### **Key Components:**
1. **Physical/spatial element:** The parks will incorporate native vegetation, rain gardens that absorb excess stormwater, spaces for community events, and walking paths connecting to the existing waterfront boardwalk.
2. **Programming/activity element:** The parks will host seasonal programs—outdoor art classes, gardening workshops, and family-friendly events—promoting both environmental education and collective stewardship.
3. **Community engagement element:** Collaborations with local schools, environmental organizations, and community volunteers will ensure the parks stay vibrant and well-maintained, also providing opportunities for local artists to contribute to the parks' design with themed installations that reflect the community's history.

### **Implementation Approach:**
- **Phase 1:** Initial community engagement will take place through town halls, where residents can share ideas and concerns about the waterfront. Early action will involve clearing and preparing the sites while assembling a team of local stakeholders to oversee the project.
- **Phase 2:** The design phase will follow, featuring workshops to involve the community in choosing park elements and layout. Construction of the parks will commence, focusing on the installation of green infrastructure to manage stormwater proactively.
- **Phase 3:** Once the parks are complete, ongoing programming will launch. The focus will be on activities promoting ecological education, sustainability practices, and social events that celebrate local identity, ensuring the parks remain vibrant and valuable in the long term.

## **STAKEHOLDER ECOSYSTEM**

### **Champions:** 
Local environmental advocates, area business leaders, and community groups like the North Star Community Center will serve as champions for the Bayfront Resilience Parks, rallying support and participation from the public.

### **Partners:** 
Essential partners will include King’s Landing's municipal government, local environmental organizations (such as the Kings’ Greens Group), schools, and cultural institutions like the King's Landing Historical Society, which can contribute historic context and cultural significance to park design.

### **Beneficiaries:** 
Residents, especially those in vulnerable and low-income areas near Blackwater Bay, stand to benefit the most as they will gain access to green spaces that enhance both their quality of life and community connectivity. Additionally, local businesses may see increased foot traffic as the parks become community destinations.

### **Potential Opposition:**
Some residents and stakeholders might resist due to concerns about potential gentrification and displacement linked to town beautification projects. To address these concerns, open communication will be prioritized, emphasizing community involvement and stewardship. Safeguards will also be established to ensure the park is maintained as a public resource for all demographics.

## **FEASIBILITY & IMPACT**

### **Success Indicators:**
- **Quantitative metric:** Increased foot traffic in the parks as measured by visitor counts within the first year of opening.
- **Qualitative metric:** Positive feedback from community surveys assessing satisfaction with park amenities and programming.
- **Community-defined metric:** Measurable participation rates in park events and volunteer days, reflecting community involvement and ownership.

### **Ripple Effects:**
The introduction of these parks will likely catalyze additional environmental literacy initiatives across King’s Landing, strengthen community ties, and inspire future projects aimed at sustainability and resilience. This initiative could also promote local biodiversity, benefiting regional wildlife and enhancing ecological stability.

### **Risk Mitigation:**
One primary risk involves project finances and funding. A comprehensive fundraising plan, including grants from environmental organizations and partnerships with local businesses, will help mitigate this risk. Community-backed initiatives will also provide a strong basis for garnering support and resources.

This project directly addresses King’s Landing's vulnerability to climate change while emphasizing local culture and community engagement. The proactive integration of green infrastructure will not only protect physical spaces but also reinforce community ties that are essential for resilience in times of environmental crisis.
